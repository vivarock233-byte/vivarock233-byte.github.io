My research interests sit at the interface of **microbial physiology**, **systems biology**, **genome-scale metabolic modeling**, and **synthetic biology**. I am especially interested in using computational models to explain why closely related microbial chassis show different growth phenotypes under different carbon sources.

## Current research highlights

### Methanol yeast phenotypic diversity and metabolic network divergence

I am building a comparative framework for methanol yeast chassis by combining **carbon-source phenotyping**, **strain-specific genome-scale metabolic model reconstruction**, and **flux-level analysis**. The goal is to identify how differences in genes and reactions may explain growth differences under methanol, glucose, xylose, and other carbon-source conditions.

### Genome-scale metabolic modeling for microbial chassis evaluation

I use constraint-based modeling approaches such as **FBA**, **pFBA**, reaction presence/absence comparison, and PCA of flux distributions to evaluate how different strains allocate metabolic capacity. I am particularly interested in making GEMs more useful for interpreting experimental growth data, rather than treating them only as simulation tools.

### Environmental and carbon-cycle modeling background

Before focusing on microbial systems, I worked on blue-carbon and marine carbon-cycle questions involving numerical models, remote-sensing data, and carbonate chemistry tools. This background shapes the way I think about microbial metabolism: as a dynamic system constrained by both biological networks and environmental context.
